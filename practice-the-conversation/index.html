Design principles

How it works

Practice, not content Learners respond to realistic situations instead of reading about them.
Behavior-based feedback Responses are evaluated against observable conversational behaviors.
Adaptive practice The next prompt changes based on the learner's performance.
What I would measure

Did the learning change performance?

Improvement over time Changes in performance across attempts.
Transfer to the workplace Use of the skills in real customer interactions.
Customer outcomes Changes in satisfaction and issue resolution.
How responses are assessed

What does a 1–4 score mean?

Responses are assessed across five skills. The prototype looks for observable behaviors in what the learner says and uses those results to shape the practice.

1
Needs development
The behavior is missing, unclear, or works against the situation.
2
Developing
The behavior is present, but incomplete, generic, or inconsistent.
3
Proficient
The behavior is clear, appropriate, and supports the decision or conversation.
4
Strong
The behavior is specific, responsive to the evidence, and helps move the situation forward.
This prototype uses rule-based language patterns as proxies for observable behaviors. In a production AI-enabled experience, responses could be evaluated more deeply for meaning, context, reasoning, and change over time.
