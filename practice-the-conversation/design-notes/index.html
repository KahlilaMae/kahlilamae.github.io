01 / Design

It started with a question.

What happens when someone knows what they should do, but struggles to do it in the moment?

The first design decision

Practice a response rather than select an answer.

Current design notes

Design choices and why they matter

  • Realistic situationsUpset Customer, Software Delay, and Performance Conversation focus on judgment and workplace communication.
  • Active practiceLearners type or speak in their own words instead of choosing a multiple-choice response.
  • Psychological safetyLow-stakes attempts, specific feedback, and opportunities to revise rather than a single right-answer test.
  • PersonalizationThe learner's name, context, choices, and progress create a more relevant experience.
  • Cognitive loadOne action at a time, clear text hierarchy, generous spacing, repetition, and restrained color.
  • AccessibilityClear language, text and speech options, readable layouts, and limited visual clutter. Formal accessibility testing remains necessary.

The learner experience

Choose a scenario→Respond in your own words→Get targeted feedback→Face new information→Respond again→Reflect

02 / Logic

Then I had to decide what happens next.

A conversation shouldn't continue in exactly the same way regardless of what someone says.

Current design notes

How the system processes a response

Typed response or speech transcript→Language and structure indicators→Five skill ratings→What worked / Try next

Not every scenario branches in the same way

ScenarioHow the next step is selected
Upset CustomerMore structured follow-up sequence; feedback changes based on assessed skills.
Software DelayInitial launch, delay, or unclear recommendation changes the next authored information.
Performance ConversationAccountability, support, or balanced approaches lead to different authored follow-ups.

Five skills, selected for each situation

ScenarioSkills assessed
Upset CustomerEmpathy, Listening, Ownership, Problem Solving & Communication
Software DelayJudgment, Risk Assessment, Perspective Taking, Problem Solving & Communication
Performance ConversationDiagnosis, Accountability, Curiosity, Judgment & Communication

Rule-based language indicators offer feedback but do not fully interpret intent or conversational nuance.

Research areas

Adult learning · Deliberate practice · Feedback · Cognitive load · Psychological safety · Universal Design for Learning · Kirkpatrick evaluation

03 / Measurement + Research

How would I know someone was getting better?

The bigger question is what would demonstrate that practice changes behavior.

Five skills · Three practice rounds · 100 possible points

Scores show performance against the prototype's criteria; the next step is to test whether those criteria reflect real workplace capability.

Current design notes

How scoring works

Average each skill across the completed rounds → round to a whole number → multiply by 4 → add the five skill totals.

04 / Business Impact

Did the learning change how people work?

I’d look for those skills in real conversations and measure their impact on business outcomes.

Current design notes

From practice to business impact

Practice→Demonstrated capability→Workplace behavior→Operational performance→Business results

I'd compare attempts, check scores against expert ratings, observe workplace application, and track relevant business outcomes.

What I'd measure next

LevelQuestionEvidence
ReactionWas practice relevant & useful?Relevance, usability & learner feedback
LearningDid demonstrated skills improve?Response quality, rubric scores & unfamiliar-scenario assessments
BehaviorDid people apply the skills at work?Observed conversations, coaching follow-up & work samples
ResultsDid changes in behavior help the organization?Role-relevant customer, leadership or operational measures
