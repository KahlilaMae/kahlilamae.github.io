01 / Design

It started with a question.

What happens when someone knows what they should do, but struggles to do it in the moment?

Design decision

Practice a response rather than select an answer.

Design choices and why they matter

  • Realistic situationsUpset Customer, Software Delay, and Performance Conversation focus on judgment and workplace communication.
  • Active practiceUsers type or speak in their own words instead of choosing a multiple-choice response.
  • Psychological safetyPeople can practice, get feedback, and try again without pressure.
  • PersonalizationUsing someone's name and responding to their choices can make the practice feel more personal.
  • Cognitive loadOne step at a time, with enough space and clear wording to focus on what matters.
  • AccessibilityI used clear language, readable layouts, and options to type or speak. Accessibility testing is still needed.

User experience

  1. Choose a scenario
  2. Respond
  3. Get feedback
  4. Face new information
  5. Respond again
  6. Reflect
  7. Review progress
  8. Set practice reminder

Reusable Format

A manager might see a skill their team needs to strengthen. I could create or adapt a scenario around that need, giving people a way to practice and receive feedback.

Manager identifies a skill gap→I create or adapt a scenario→Team members practice and receive feedback→Manager checks job performance

02 / Logic

How the Expereince Responds?

Design decision

I wanted each response to matter, so I built different follow-ups based on what someone says.

How the system processes a response

Typed response or speech transcript→Language and structure indicators→Five skill ratings→What worked / Try next

How scenarios branch differently

ScenarioHow the next step is selected
Upset CustomerMore structured follow-up sequence; feedback changes based on assessed skills.
Software DelayInitial launch, delay, or unclear recommendation changes the next authored information.
Performance ConversationAccountability, support, or balanced approaches lead to different authored follow-ups.

03 / Business Impact

What improved?

How do I know whether someone is actually getting better?

Design decision

Practice scores tell me part of the story. I want to know whether people are doing something differently at work.

From practice to business impact

Practice→What someone learns→What they do differently→What changes at work→The value created

I'd look for improvement in practice, but I wouldn't stop there. I'd want to understand what people carry into their work, and where they might still need support.

What I'd measure next

LevelQuestionEvidence
ReactionWas practice relevant & useful?Relevance, usability & user feedback
LearningDid demonstrated skills improve?Response quality, rubric scores & unfamiliar-scenario assessments
BehaviorDid people apply the skills at work?Observed conversations, coaching follow-up & work samples
ResultsDid changes in behavior help the organization?Role-relevant customer, leadership or operational measures

Individual practice results could also help identify patterns across a team. If several people struggle with the same skill, managers could use that information to identify development needs and provide more targeted support.

How scoring works

The prototype measures five skills across up to three practice rounds, with 100 possible points.

Scores are averaged across completed rounds, whether someone finishes one, two, or three. Incomplete rounds are excluded.

The scores tell me how someone performed in practice. I still need to test what they mean in real situations.

04 / Future Directions

What’s next?

I'm curious whether more personal feedback could help someone respond differently when a real conversation becomes difficult.

Design decision

Explore how an LLM could make feedback more individualized without replacing human judgment.

Feedback report card

The feedback report card is meant to start a conversation, not end the learning experience. Team members and managers could reflect together on what worked, what could improve, and one behavior to practice next. I'd want to know whether that conversation leads to someone doing something differently at work.

How the LLM would fit

I'd keep the learning design in place and use an LLM to make feedback and follow-up questions more responsive. The model would receive the user's response and skill rubric, while the app would check the feedback before displaying it.

User types or speaks→App sends the response and scenario context to a secure backend→Backend calls the LLM with the skill rubric and instructions→LLM suggests feedback and a follow-up→App checks and displays the next step

LLM-Supported Adaptive Learning

Responsive practice→ Individualized feedback→ Try again→ Reflect

I want to understand whether the skills people develop through practice lead to meaningful changes in how they work. I'd also want to know whether those changes support business priorities and improve the bottom line.

Evaluation and safeguards

I'd set clear instructions for how the LLM should respond, check its feedback, and have a backup if its response was unclear or inappropriate.

!User responses would need clear privacy and retention rules before this could be tested with people.

Human evaluationI'd compare LLM-generated feedback with expert ratings to evaluate its accuracy, consistency, and usefulness.
LLM limitationsThe LLM could make practice more responsive and individualized, but shouldn't be the only judge of someone's skills.
Continued monitoringRegularly check for scoring errors and bias, with human review when feedback is unclear or inconsistent.

!I'd partner with a machine learning engineer to integrate the LLM into the learning experience.
